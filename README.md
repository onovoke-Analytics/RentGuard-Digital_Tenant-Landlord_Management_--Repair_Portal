RentGuard-Digital_Tenant-Landlord_Management_Repair_Portal

A research-driven analysis of tenant and landlord experiences, rental payment management, maintenance and repairs, communication, record keeping, and the potential adoption of an integrated digital rental-management platform.

Introduction

The rental housing sector involves continuous interactions between tenants, landlords and property managers, particularly in rent collection, payment confirmation, maintenance, repairs, communication and documentation.

However, many of these activities are managed through separate manual and digital channels, creating challenges relating to transparency, accountability, record keeping and operational efficiency.

The RentGuard project investigates these challenges using survey data to determine how an integrated digital platform could improve rental-management processes and the overall tenant–landlord experience.

Chart / Dashboard

The analysis was developed using Microsoft Power BI, comprising five interconnected dashboard sections:

1.	Overview Dashboard: Respondent demographics, geographical distribution, gender and employment status.
2.	Tenant Management & Repair Portal: Rent payment methods, receipt tracking, maintenance challenges, communication and repair resolution.
3.	Tenant Management & Repair Portal II: Property types, maintenance frustrations, repair satisfaction, notification preferences and common maintenance issues.
4.	Landlord Management Portal: Property management practices, repair coordination, record keeping, rent collection challenges and administrative workload.
5.	User Digital Readiness & Adoption: Digital readiness, perceived application efficiency, preferred features, adoption and recommendation likelihood.

Story of the Data

The RentGuard research began with an electronic questionnaire designed to understand the experiences and expectations of tenants and landlords/property managers.

The original survey generated 152 response records. Following data-quality checks, one incomplete timestamp-only submission was excluded, leaving 151 valid respondents across 20 Nigerian states.

The analysis revealed three major issues:
1.	Maintenance inefficiency: Tenants experience delayed repairs, poor communication and inadequate maintenance-status updates.
2.	Fragmented financial documentation: Although digital rent payments are widely used, payment receipts and rental records remain distributed across different channels.
3.	Administrative challenges: Landlords experience difficulties with rent reminders, maintenance coordination and manual record keeping.

The findings provide an evidence-based foundation for developing RentGuard as a unified digital rental-management solution.

Methodology

The research adopted a quantitative descriptive survey approach, supported by limited qualitative information from open-ended responses.

The methodology followed these stages:

1.	Data Collection: Gathering primary data through an electronic questionnaire administered to tenants and landlords/property managers.
2.	Data Preparation: Reviewing the raw dataset for accuracy, consistency, completeness and suitability for analysis.
3.	Data Cleaning: Identifying incomplete records, reviewing duplicates, standardizing categories, handling missing values and distinguishing role-specific responses.
4.	Data Validation: Confirming the final analytical population, reviewing data types and ensuring consistency between the original and cleaned datasets.
5.	Data Analysis: Examining respondent demographics, rental payment patterns, maintenance challenges, communication, record keeping and digital adoption preferences.
6.	Data Visualization: Using Power BI to develop interactive dashboards, KPIs, charts, geographical maps and comparative visualizations.

The analysis was primarily descriptive and exploratory, focusing on identifying patterns and generating actionable insights rather than establishing causal relationships.

In-Analysis Observations and Statistical Findings

The project revealed the following major findings across five key areas:

1. Rental Payment and Documentation: The analysis revealed that approximately 95% of tenants use bank transfers or mobile banking for rent payments, indicating a high level of digital payment adoption. However, payment documentation remains fragmented, as 58 tenants still rely on physical paper receipts, while 28 depend solely on bank alerts for payment confirmation. Furthermore, 143 respondents (94.7%) reported yearly rent payment or collection as the predominant payment arrangement. 

These findings suggest that although digital payment channels are widely adopted, there is a need for automated digital receipts, payment verification and centralized transaction histories to improve transparency and reduce potential payment disputes.

2. Maintenance and Repair Challenges: Maintenance emerged as one of the major operational challenges identified by the respondents. A total of 67 tenants (58%) identified delayed landlord responses or repairs as their major maintenance challenge, while 60 tenants reported that maintenance issues took more than one week to resolve. Plumbing was the most frequently reported maintenance problem, with 45 responses, followed by water supply issues with 38 responses and electrical faults with 24 responses. 

These findings highlight significant gaps in maintenance coordination and timely repairs, reinforcing the need for a structured maintenance-reporting system that enables tenants to submit complaints, monitor repair progress and receive timely updates.

3. Communication and Maintenance Frustration: The analysis showed that maintenance communication is predominantly conducted through informal channels, with phone calls and WhatsApp/text messages accounting for approximately 40% each. Physical visits represented 19.13%, while emails accounted for approximately 0.87%. Additionally, 66 tenants identified slow landlord responses or delayed repairs as their major source of frustration. This indicates that communication between tenants and landlords is largely fragmented across different channels, potentially limiting effective monitoring and accountability. 

A centralized communication and maintenance-tracking system could therefore improve information sharing, response monitoring and accessibility of maintenance records.

4. Landlord Management Challenges: The landlord analysis revealed that rent reminders, maintenance coordination and record keeping constitute significant administrative challenges. A total of 26 landlords identified reminding tenants about rent due dates as their major rent-collection challenge, while 52.78% still rely on physical notebooks for record keeping. Furthermore, managing repairs and communicating with tenants were identified as the most time-consuming administrative activities, with 11 responses each. Another 27 landlords reported manually forwarding repair information to technicians through phone calls or text messages. 

These findings demonstrate the extent of manual administrative processes in rental management and highlight the potential benefits of automated rent reminders, centralized property records, digital documentation and integrated maintenance coordination.

5. Digital Readiness and Adoption: The digital readiness and adoption analysis revealed encouraging prospects for the proposed RentGuard platform. The dashboard recorded an overall digital readiness indicator of 77%, while 85% of respondents indicated that a unified application could improve rental-management efficiency. Furthermore, the dashboard recorded 68% as the likelihood of adopting RentGuard and 67% as the likelihood of recommending the platform. Supporting findings from the research documentation showed that 145 respondents (96%) agreed or strongly agreed that downloadable digital receipts and records are essential, while 139 respondents (92.1%) supported online payment tracking and an equal number supported live repair tracking. Additionally, 63 respondents selected a clean dashboard for financial history and lease documents as their top-priority feature, while maintenance/repair history and rent collection/financial summaries received 61 and 60 responses respectively. 

These findings indicate substantial interest in an integrated digital rental-management solution, particularly in payment transparency, digital documentation, maintenance monitoring and financial reporting. They also provide useful evidence for prioritizing the features to be incorporated into RentGuard's Minimum Viable Product (MVP).

Overall Observation: The analysis establishes that the major challenges in rental management are not limited to payment collection but extend to maintenance delays, fragmented communication, inadequate documentation and manual administrative processes. The findings therefore support the development of RentGuard as an integrated digital platform designed to improve transparency, accountability and operational efficiency between tenants and landlords.

Statistical interpretation note: The adoption and recommendation percentages represent stated intentions and dashboard indicators, not actual platform usage or proven product performance. The findings should therefore be validated through subsequent MVP development and pilot testing.

Recommendations

Based on the findings, the following recommendations are proposed:

1. Implement digital payment management: Introduce automated rent reminders, payment tracking, downloadable receipts and transaction histories.
2. Develop a maintenance tracking system: Provide structured maintenance requests, repair assignments, status updates and completion records.
3. Integrate communication channels: Support in-app notifications, WhatsApp, SMS and email while retaining a centralized communication history.
4. Introduce centralized property records: Maintain accessible tenant information, lease documents, property records and payment histories.
5. Automate landlord administration: Provide rent collection monitoring, reminders, maintenance coordination and financial reporting.
6. Prioritize user-friendly design: Develop a simple, mobile-friendly interface with guided onboarding to accommodate users with different levels of digital readiness.
7. Conduct a controlled pilot: Test the minimum viable product (MVP) with selected tenants and landlords, measuring actual adoption, payment recording, receipt retrieval, maintenance response times and repeat usage.

Overall recommendation: Prioritize five interconnected MVP components: property and tenancy records, digital payment and receipts, maintenance tracking, communication and notifications, and role-specific reporting dashboards.

Concluding Statement

The RentGuard analysis demonstrates that rental-management activities face challenges relating to maintenance delays, fragmented payment documentation, communication gaps and manual administrative processes.

The findings reveal strong respondent interest in digital payment tracking, automated receipts, live repair monitoring and integrated property-management reporting.

Consequently, RentGuard presents a promising opportunity to improve transparency, accountability and operational efficiency in tenant–landlord relationships. However, its effectiveness and actual adoption must be established through MVP development, controlled pilot testing and continuous performance evaluation.
